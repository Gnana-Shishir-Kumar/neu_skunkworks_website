§ 01

PREMISE

What is a skunkworks?

FIELD MANUAL / ENTRY 001

DECLASSIFIED · OPEN ACCESS

A skunkworks project is one built by a small, loosely-structured team whose only job is radical innovation. The term comes from Lockheed's secret aircraft division — they built the U-2 in eight months in 1955 in a rented circus tent next to a plastics factory.

Ours is the student version. No actual skunks involved. What translates is the operating model: small teams, short loops, real stakes, and the freedom to be wrong in interesting ways.

We run research groups, open-mic nights, mentorship pairings, workshops, seminars, and hackathons. Membership is open to anyone at Northeastern willing to do the work.

§ 02

OPERATIONAL LAYERS

What the work actually is.

CARD 01 · LAYER · TOOLING

Learn the machines

TIER 01 — NECESSARY, NOT SUFFICIENT

Frameworks, libraries, training pipelines, deployment. The table stakes. Required, but not the reason you joined a skunkworks. Machines are already superhuman at this. Operate the forklift; don't compete with it.

CARD 02 · LAYER · RESEARCH

Push the edges

TIER 02 — WHERE WE LIVE

Six active research groups: causal inference, explainable AI, observability, visual analytics, generative art, and competition ML. Student-led, faculty-advised, mentor-supported. Real papers, real deployments, real failures.

CARD 03 · LAYER · JUDGMENT

Decide what's worth building

TIER 03 — THE IRREDUCIBLE PART

The thing AI cannot supply: problem formulation, taste, the call about what's worth your semester. The reason a skunkworks exists at all. Practical wisdom under real stakes — no model has any.

§ 04

CHARTER

What we optimize for.

We do not build features.

We build the things that make features look obvious in retrospect.

Taste is the only moat that doesn't decay.

  1. Ship the experiment, not the product. A semester is long enough to learn something irreversible.
  2. Compute is cheap. Conviction is rare. GPUs are rented; the call on what to run is not.
  3. Read the paper, then ignore it. Literature is a map, not a destination.
  4. Small teams, short loops. If the standup needs a standup, the team is too large.
  5. Disagree in private. Commit in public. Dissent is fuel; fragmentation is failure.
  6. The bug is the discovery. Every broken pipeline is a hypothesis in disguise.
