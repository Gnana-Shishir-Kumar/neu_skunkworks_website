§ 01.A — Research Divisions

Six groups. One culture.

DIV.001 Faculty TBA · Lead Members: 8 Meets: Tuesdays 6pm

Causal Inference

What actually caused what? Statistical models find correlation; causal inference asks the harder question. The group works through Pearl's hierarchy, runs do-calculus exercises, and applies counterfactual methods to real datasets — currently a clinical decision-making collaboration with Mass General.

  • DO-CALCULUS
  • COUNTERFACTUALS
  • PROPENSITY
  • DAGs
  • INSTRUMENTAL VARS
DIV.002 Lead: TBA Members: 12 Meets: Thursdays 5pm

Explainable AI

Model interpretability is no longer optional. The group surveys and implements XAI methods — SHAP, LIME, integrated gradients, attention rollouts — and audits real models. A 2019 project from this group was deployed at Squark.ai. Multiple papers in preparation.

  • SHAP
  • LIME
  • ATTENTION
  • MODEL CARDS
  • AUDIT
DIV.003 Lead: TBA Members: 6 Meets: Fridays 4pm

Visual Analytics

Where information design meets machine learning. Builds interactive visualization tools that surface what models are doing — and lets domain experts disagree with them in a structured way. Cross-listed with the Information Design and Visualization program.

  • D3
  • OBSERVABLE
  • INTERACTION DESIGN
  • USER STUDIES
DIV.004 Lead: TBA Members: 5 Meets: Wednesdays 7pm

ML Observability

Production models drift, fail silently, and lie about their confidence. The group builds instrumentation: drift detectors, calibration audits, fairness monitors. The unsexy work that decides whether a model survives contact with reality.

  • DRIFT
  • CALIBRATION
  • MONITORING
  • MLOPS
DIV.005 Lead: TBA Members: 9 Meets: Mondays 6pm — open to all levels

Kaggle Group

Competition-driven learning. Pick a Kaggle competition each semester, work it as a team across feature engineering, modeling, and ensembling. Currently working the RSNA Pulmonary Embolism Detection challenge.

  • PYTORCH
  • XGBOOST
  • FEATURE ENG
  • CV
  • ENSEMBLING
DIV.006 Lead: TBA Members: 7 Meets: Tuesdays 8pm

Gauguin

Generative art research. Named for the painter who left the Paris market to make work the market wasn't asking for. Studies aesthetic judgment as a research problem — what makes generative output good rather than merely novel. Outputs: exhibitions, papers, the occasional album.

  • DIFFUSION
  • GAN
  • AESTHETIC EVAL
  • CREATIVE CODING
  • TYPOGRAPHY
